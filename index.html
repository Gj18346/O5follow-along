Tennis - History

Where it came from

Around the 11th or 12th century, French monks began playing a handball-style game
called jeu de paume ("game of the palm"). Instead of rackets, players struck a cloth or
leather ball with their bare hands—and later, leather gloves—over a rope strung across
monastery courtyards.

The word "tennis" comes from the Old French word tenez, which means
"take this" or "be ready". Servers would shout this as a fair warning to their opponents
before hitting the ball

How it developed

Following Major Wingfield's 1873 lawn tennis patent, the sport grew rapidly among the
upper class. The "Four Grand Slam" nations established their major tournaments in quick
succession, standardizing the global rules

Why its the best sport ever

Tennis requires an extraordinary combination of athletic traits. A player must possess the
explosive sprinting of a track athlete, the stamina of a marathon runner, the hand-eye
coordination of a baseball player, and the flexibility of a gymnast.
Mentally, it is incredibly brutal. Because tennis has no game clock, a player cannot simply
"run out the clock" to win. You must win the final point yourself. This creates massive
psychological pressure, as a player trailing by a huge margin can theoretically still launch
a comeback and win.
